The garden two friends painted

Jardin du Luxembourg · the gates close at 18:15 in late October; listen from the railings on rue Guynemer, or later

Spot the Matisse before you read the captions: two friends, the same alleys, the same year.

▶ Listen · The garden turned into Tahiti · 4 min

From about 1898 Matisse and Albert Marquet, friends since Gustave Moreau's studio, painted outdoors together in this garden, young and broke. Matisse's alley has a tall red tree, two pink trees and blue shadows; no palace, no basin, no statue. The Russian collector Shchukin bought it in 1906, and it hangs in Saint Petersburg, where the curator says it was painted under Gauguin's Polynesian influence and a scholar remarked that without the signature you would hardly take it for Paris. He turned the Luxembourg into Tahiti. A second Matisse view of the garden, from 1903, was stolen from a museum in Rio in 2006 and never found.

At the garden's north-west corner stood the State's museum of living artists. In 1922 the State bought a Matisse for it, an odalisque painted in Nice: seventeen years after the wild beasts, the first Matisse officially bought for a Paris museum.

Fun fact

Others painted here too: a German student of Matisse in 1910, a Japanese painter in 1927, and the Turkish painter Namık İsmail, whose 1924 picture of the fountain at the garden's far end hangs in Istanbul.
